# What the morning's work established

## 1. B1 — Minimal Stickman
The first procrastination preview had stray lines and unclear laptop/character placement. The accepted revision established one desk layout, a real rear over-shoulder master and matching screen/coffee/pencil/phone inserts. That spatial consistency is the core of this style. Twelve purposeful shots follow a complete Fola recording and finish with continuing work, not a decorative zoom. Clean contours and contact-specific hand poses matter more than additional line detail. The historical implementation used the B1 Canvas/runtime pipeline; future masters use Hyperframes.

## 2. A5 — Historical Cartoon
The Suriname film uses adult white-headed stick-cartoon anatomy dressed in period clothes, with rich painterly places. The edit explains historical processes through people, settings and object inserts. Script research and respectful representation are part of direction. The opening and later full film used the then-selected Mini model, but the ledger exposed unwanted book/hand motion. Preserve the style and reference film; retire the old engine. Current Wan/authored routing needs its own shot proof and must not inherit a claim of validation from Mini.

## 3. Expressive Cartoon — Generated and Hybrid
The first conventional human treatment was rejected. The revised large-eye, oversized-head cartoon style was accepted, with locked clothing, screen sides and rear phone framing. Six Wan takes produced the first animation; trimming removed later invented actions. The approved 70/30 version retained two acting clips and replaced the rest with controlled local animation, including a rigid door hinge. These are separate workflow variants of the same visual treatment, preserved together so they can be compared without confusing model choice with drawing style.

## 4. Illustrated Cartoon — Fully Authored
The next test kept those illustrated characters but used no generated footage. Local face cels, eye patches, torso deformation and a hand/door mechanism produced a controlled music-led short. It is highly repeatable once artwork is registered. It is also limited: walking and broad gestural acting were not built. This deserves a separate skill from the hybrid route because its asset preparation, action budget and failure modes are different.

## 5. Capsule Cinema — Fully Authored and Articulated
The supplied LUST film introduced a different drawing language: small eyes, cream capsule bodies, sparse dark stages, a magenta habit metaphor and warm dawn. The condensed recreation uses native JavaScript geometry and articulated body poses. Push-up hand/toe anchors and sitting contact were approved. The drawer's return arm was then corrected: preserve contact, author the elbow/hand arc, keep the wrist clear, settle beside the thigh. The user approved v2 and requested saving it. This is the most recent rigging benchmark, not a blanket claim that every future action is already rigged.

## Shared lessons
A storyboard must specify actual camera geography and contacts. Locked reference art preserves identity; it does not guarantee generated motion. Exact mechanical actions often belong in authored animation. A still with a camera move is not character acting. Rigging is judged by the silhouette and transitions, not only numerical joint constraints. A zero-provider-cost render still requires authoring, art and QA. Existing cast/story approvals do not automatically approve an unseen longer film.

## Comparison limits
These were successive practical tests, not a controlled model benchmark. Resolutions, source durations, references and soundtracks changed across some tests. The 70/30 allocation is finished screen time, not paid source time. The full-Wan/hybrid examples use Fola; the authored examples use Suno and Foley. Costs and subjective quality should be compared with those differences explicit.
