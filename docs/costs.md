# Cost records are dated examples, not live quotes

At the session's $0.005/credit conversion, holding one new 12-credit Suno request constant: all-Wan brothers equivalent $2.46, 70/30 equivalent $0.94, fully authored illustrated $0.06. The hybrid actually reused existing video, and its 9 finished Wan seconds came from 11 billed source seconds. Built-in image metering was not exposed. Code, rigs, review, subscriptions and rejected output are additional.

The six-second Omni ledger test cost 84 credits ($0.42). The stricter Mini test cost 22.8 credits ($0.114), at a different resolution tier. Both failed strict compliance; Mini is retired. Do not use those prices as a future routing recommendation.

The latest capsule recreation and rig refinement spent no additional provider credits: native vector art/code, existing music and Foley. That does not price authoring labor.

For 30 four-minute films, the planning asset/audio allowance was $150–250: episode images $90–150, reusable rig artwork $20–30, Suno $10.80–21.60, effects $20–40. It assumed $0.05/image as an explicit Kie 2K benchmark, not the measured price of the built-in image tool. A native-vector approach can avoid those image charges while increasing authoring work.

Rig artwork is not rig programming. An illustrative outsourced scenario at $50/hour assumed 24–40 initial rig hours plus 8–16 hours per film: $13,350–26,250 including the asset allowance. Those hours are unvalidated planning assumptions, not an established production rate or quote. Measure a representative four-minute pilot before pricing a batch. Recheck current provider models, prices and balances when needed; no account balances are stored here as live capacity.
