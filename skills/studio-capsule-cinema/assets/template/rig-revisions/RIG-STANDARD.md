# Character rig review standard

Established after the user's drawer-arm feedback. Keep the approved character design, push-up mechanics and seated rooftop pose as references for future episodes. Do not start the longer film until its brief is supplied.

1. Author neutral, contact, release and final rest drawings before interpolation. A solved joint position is not automatically a good drawing.
2. Keep separate controls for the arm's contact constraint and its return animation. Lock the prop contact until deliberate release, then use a planned arc.
3. Calibrate shoulder, elbow, wrist and palm anchors; choose the elbow bend side explicitly. Inspect the full silhouette with the torso visible.
4. The resting hand should sit alongside the thigh, with a relaxed wrist and a slight elbow bend. Avoid folded-across-body rest poses unless the action requires one.
5. Reuse approved hand shapes. Additional finger detail or wrist patches must improve the drawing at delivery size, not create joints or seams.
6. For 2D view changes, define the projected proportions intentionally. Do not let unconstrained reach or singularities stretch or flip the arm.
7. Review each contact and release at consecutive frames, then at normal speed. Inspect shoulders, elbows, fingers, prop clearance and planted feet. Numerical checks supplement visual review.
8. Compare approved shots before/after a shared-rig edit. A local fix must not change approved acting elsewhere.
9. Keep the previous video and rig source for comparison; export through Hyperframes. Approval of this pose does not automatically approve new actions in the longer film.
